Before you start

What you need and installing

What you need

A Windows PC
With an NVIDIA or AMD graphics card, and your VR games installed.
An Apple Vision Pro
On a current visionOS.
One home network
PC and headset on the same network. A wired PC and a Wi-Fi 6 or 6E router in the same room give the steadiest picture.

Install on the PC

Download the installer from the latest release, run it and follow it through. Windows asks for permission once. Then open KRVR from the Start menu.

Install on the headset

Open the App Store on your Vision Pro, search for KRVR and tap Get. Open it and allow what it asks for: world sensing places the picture in your room, hand tracking is for hand input and the passthrough windows. If you play SteamVR games, run SteamVR room setup on the PC once.

Linux and SteamOS (testing)

Open the latest release and download the file for your system from the Linux and SteamOS section:

SteamOS or Steam Deck
The SteamOS .tar.gz file. Run KRVR from Desktop Mode.
Ubuntu, Debian, Mint, Pop
The .deb file.
Fedora
The .rpm file.
Arch or Manjaro
The .pkg.tar.zst file.
Anything else
The Flatpak file.

Not sure your PC is ready? Run Linux-readiness-check.sh from the same page first. Step by step notes are in LINUX-INSTALL.md there. Send feedback and your Diagnose report in the #linux-steamos channel on Discord.

Connect your Vision Pro to your PC

Do these in order the first time. After that the headset remembers your PC and Connect is all you need. You need the KRVR server on your Windows PC, the KRVR app on your Vision Pro, and both on the same Wi-Fi.

Open KRVR and press Start

Open the KRVR window on your PC and press the blue Start button. The dot turns green and it says Running. The headset can't connect until this is done. The first time, Windows may ask whether to allow KRVR on your network: press Allow.

Part 2

The PC window

The KRVR window on your PC. Most people only ever press Start. Everything else is here when you want it.

Quality

Resolution4096

Resolution
How sharp the game draws. Higher is sharper but harder on your graphics card. Lower it if a game stutters. next game launch
FoV scale
How much of the view the game draws. Below 100% is narrower and sharper. next game launch
IPD
The distance between your eyes. If the world feels too small, lower it. Too big, raise it. 64 suits most people.

Stream

Encoder preset
P1 is fastest with the lowest delay. Higher numbers give a cleaner picture but take longer. Keep P1 unless your graphics card has power to spare.
10-bit encode
Less banding in skies and dark scenes. Leave it on. live
Graphics card
NVIDIA or AMD. Only change it if the wrong one is shown.
OpenXR runtime
KRVR streams OpenXR games straight to the headset. SteamVR runs them through SteamVR instead.
Start with PC
Opens KRVR and starts the server when you sign in to Windows.

Sound and mic

Audio output
Headset plays game sound in the headset and mutes the PC. Desktop keeps it on the PC. live
Game audio
Default output sends everything you hear. VR game sends only the game. live
Sound in the headset
On the headset, Settings, Sound. Stereo plays the game's own mix straight to your ears. Spatial places it around you. live
Headset mic
Your Vision Pro's microphone as a mic on the PC, for voice chat and team radio. Mute it any time from the pill.

Microphone, one-time setup. Windows games can only use a microphone device, so the headset mic needs a free virtual cable:

1
Download VB-Cable (free), run its installer as administrator and restart if it asks. KRVR offers the link itself the first time it finds no cable.
2
Leave Headset mic in KRVR on Auto. It finds the cable by itself.
3
In your game, Discord or team radio, choose CABLE Output as the microphone.

If CABLE Input isn't listed after installing, Windows left it disabled: open Sound settings, Recording devices, right-click CABLE Input and choose Enable.

Moving and sizing the pill

Tap the pill and use the bar that appears under it to move it. Interface size in Settings, Pill bar sets how big the pill and Settings window are. Cover size sets the game covers separately.

Your PC desktop

The desktop icon shows your PC screen in the headset. Point with your hand, pinch to click, pinch and drag to move the cursor. The bar under the panel moves it.

What the stats mean

FPS
The game's frame rate.
HZ
The rate reaching the headset.
M2P
Motion to photon: the time from moving your head to seeing it.
ENC, AIR, DEC, DISP
The steps inside M2P: encode on the PC, Wi-Fi, decode on the headset, wait for the display.
LATE
Frames that arrived late this second. 0 is healthy.
HEAT
How warm the headset is running.
Part 4

Games

Your PC's games, as covers in the headset.

Two kinds of game, both handled for you

OpenXR games
Run straight into KRVR. Pick OpenXR in the game's launch options if it asks. Le Mans Ultimate, iRacing, DCS and Microsoft Flight Simulator are OpenXR.
SteamVR games
Start through SteamVR as usual. Half-Life: Alyx, VRChat and No Man's Sky are SteamVR.

Connect first, then start the game. The first minute is loading and settling, so judge the picture once you're actually playing.

Start a game
Hold a cover. The game starts on your PC and streams to you.
Stop a game
Hold the cover of the running game again.
Search
Find a game by name.
Which games show
Choose them on the PC in the Games window.

SteamVR games need SteamVR room setup done once on the PC. Your height comes from the headset, so there's nothing else to calibrate.

Eye tracking in games

Nothing to turn on, in the headset or on the PC. KRVR always tracks your eyes for its foveated streaming, so any game with eye tracking can use it as soon as you're connected, in both OpenXR and SteamVR games. Just switch on eye tracking in the game's own settings.

VRChat
Turn on OSC in VRChat's settings and your avatar's eyes follow yours.
Face tracking
On the headset, Settings, Controllers, Face tracking On, and allow the camera. In VRChat, with OSC on and an avatar that supports it, your avatar's eyes, blinks, mouth, lips and tongue follow yours.
Part 5 · Settings > Quality

Picture quality

KRVR sends your eyes full detail where you look and less towards the edges, following your gaze. That's how a sharp picture fits over Wi-Fi.

Foveation

◎Foveation
The circle is where you are looking.
Stream 1696 × 1360 per eye
Fovea size9°
How big the sharp area around where you look is.
Periphery6
How soft the picture gets outside the sharp area.
Fovea resolution130%
Cleaner pixels inside the gaze area.

The picture shows what the sliders do, with your gaze on the middle gauge. Green ticks mark the recommended setting.

Fovea size
Bigger if you notice blur when you glance around. live
Periphery
Lower if the sides of your view look soft. live
Fovea resolution
Cleaner pixels inside the gaze area. For more detail, raise Resolution on the PC. restart the game
Stream
The size of the picture sent to your headset. It changes as you move the sliders.

Bigger settings make a bigger stream, and your headset has a fixed amount of memory for the video. The bar next to the stream size shows how much it uses, and the sliders stop before it runs out, because past that the picture would not show. 10-bit (on the PC) gives smoother colour but each frame takes twice the memory, so the sliders stop sooner; with 10-bit off they go further. If the stream size turns red you're above recommended: your headset may run hot and slow down. Stream size changes apply when the game restarts.

Bitrate, sharpen and picture

Bitrate
Leave it on Auto. It follows what your Wi-Fi and headset can carry and shows the rate it's using. Manual lets you set it yourself.
Sharpen
Crisper detail. live
Brightness, Contrast, Saturation
Adjust the picture to taste. live
FOV Crop
Trims edges of the view you can't see through the lenses, so the game draws less. Bring each line in until it sits just past what you can see. next game launch
Part 6 · Settings > Performance

Smoothness

Motion Smoothing OffOn
On: when a game can't keep up, in-between frames are made so motion stays fluid. Best for demanding sims. Off: every frame the game makes is shown as it is. live
Frame delivery SmoothFast
Smooth holds one frame in hand for the steadiest motion. Fast shows each frame the moment it arrives, a little sooner. live
Vibration smoothing OffAutoManual
For seat shakers, bass shakers and motion rigs: steadies the view when your seat makes the headset vibrate. Auto finds the right amount by itself; Manual gives you a slider, raise it until the bar stays low. Leave it Off if nothing vibrates. live
Video link UDPTCP
How the picture travels. UDP, the default: a lost piece is repaired when possible, otherwise that frame is dropped and a new one asked for. TCP: a lost piece is sent again and the picture waits for it. Switches mid-game. live
Refresh rate (M5)
90 Hz or 120 Hz. 120 Hz is smoother on an M5 Vision Pro when the game can hold 120 fps. Use Mixed immersion for 120 Hz: in Full, Vision Pro usually keeps the display at 90. live

Part 8 · Settings > Passthrough

Your real room

See parts of your real room inside the game. Turning any of these on lets your room show through.

Immersion

Full
Hides your room completely.
Mixed
Keeps your room behind the game. Needed for masks, and for 120 Hz.
Progressive
The Digital Crown fades your room in and out.

Change it with the view icon on the pill.

Cockpit

A window onto your wheel and the gear around it. Press Place, rest both hands on the wheel, and it locks on after a 3-2-1. It stays fixed in your room. Size, position and softness are on its page, and the wheel icon on the pill hides and shows it.

Helmet

The inside of a GT, Formula or pilot helmet around your view, over the game and your room. The helmet icon on the pill puts it on and takes it off. On the headset, Settings, Passthrough, Helmet shapes it, and a button on your wheel or stick can switch it. The pilot helmet has night vision.

Hand discs

A window to your real hands at each wrist, so you can find a keyboard, a drink or your gear stick. Turn Merge on and the two discs join when your hands come together.

Masks

A window in the game that shows the real thing behind it, like your keyboard. On the Masks page press Add a mask, name it and pick Box or Freehand.

Box
Pinch the orb onto the thing, press Place here, then drag the corner dots to fit.
Freehand
Press Start drawing and trace round it with the pencil.
Fine tune
Move, Orient and Shape, then Save.

Roomscale

Draw your play space once: press Draw, then at each corner pull the right trigger or pinch and hold. A green post marks each corner. Press Done. Near your edge the room fades in, and the game returns when you step back.

Height

Your height comes from the headset. The Height slider in Settings is a comfort offset, for example sitting down in a standing game.

Part 9

When something's wrong

1

Can't connect

See the Connect steps, including the four fixes when it bounces back.

2

Picture soft or blocky

Leave Bitrate on Auto. If it still breaks up, raise Periphery or lower Fovea size so fewer bits go to the edges. Keep the headset on 5 GHz Wi-Fi, not 2.4 GHz, and the PC on a cable.

3

Game stutters

Lower Resolution on the PC, or turn Motion Smoothing on.

4

Controllers feel off

Set Prediction to Manual and adjust by feel. In SteamVR games the PC handles it.

5

Game opens on the monitor, not in the headset

Connect before starting the game. OpenXR games: another VR app may have taken over, so set OpenXR runtime back to KRVR on the PC. SteamVR games: SteamVR should show KRVR as the headset; if not, restart SteamVR after connecting.

6

Stuck connecting

Press Stop then Start on the PC, then Connect again.

7

A game looks odd the first time

Some games draw in unusual ways. KRVR measures and corrects them each session, which can take a few seconds the first time.

8

No sound in the headset

Set Audio output to Headset on the PC, and check Game audio points at the device the game plays through.

9

Installer says files are still the old version

SteamVR or a game was holding them. Close them and run the installer again. If it persists, restart the PC and run the installer before opening anything else.

10

Taking the headset off

KRVR pauses. Put it back on and you carry on where you left off, in the same spot.

Getting help

Every session is logged on the PC, the headset side included. Press Diagnose in the PC window and send the report in our Discord, with roughly when the problem happened.
